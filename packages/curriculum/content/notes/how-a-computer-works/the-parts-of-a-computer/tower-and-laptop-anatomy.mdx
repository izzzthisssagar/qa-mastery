Every app you will ever test runs on a physical box somewhere — and the first time a
teammate says *"just plug it into the tower"*, you want to know exactly what they mean.
Don't just read this topic — **tap, explore and collect every part** as you go.
</Hook>

<Callout type="analogy">
A computer is like a **restaurant kitchen**. The tower (or laptop body) is the kitchen
itself; inside there's a head chef (the CPU), counter space for work in progress (RAM),
and a pantry for storage (the disk). The monitor, keyboard and mouse are the serving
window — how orders come in and dishes go out.
</Callout>

## The two shapes a computer comes in

Almost every computer you'll meet at work is either a **desktop** — a separate box (the <Term define="The upright case that holds the actual computer: motherboard, CPU, RAM, disks and power supply.">tower</Term>) plus monitor, keyboard and mouse — or a **laptop**, the same parts folded into one portable case.

The important idea: **they are the same machine inside.** A laptop is a desktop that
learned to travel. Prove it to yourself below — explore both.

<WhereToCheck>
Verify what hardware you have without opening anything — let the system tell you:

- **Windows:** Settings → System → About shows the machine's name, CPU and RAM.
- **macOS:**  menu → About This Mac.
- **Any laptop:** the model number is on the sticker underneath — that's the string to search for drivers or a manual.

This habit — don't guess about the environment, **check it** — is your first real QA
skill. Bug reports say "on what machine" for a reason.
</WhereToCheck>

<Callout type="mistake">
Calling the **monitor** "the computer". The screen is only a display — the computer is
the tower (or laptop body). If a teammate says "restart the computer" and you switch the
monitor off and on, nothing restarts. Classic beginner mix-up; now it's one you'll never make.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip">
Testers meet hardware in bug reports. "Works on my machine" often decodes to a hardware
difference: less RAM, a different screen size, no dedicated graphics. Knowing the part
names lets you write an environment line like
<em>"Reproduced on a 14-inch laptop, 8 GB RAM, external monitor attached."</em>
</Callout>

<Challenge>
Write down for the machine you're using right now: **(1)** desktop or laptop, **(2)** where
its power button is, **(3)** how many USB ports it has, **(4)** where the air vents are,
**(5)** its exact model (About This Mac / Settings → About / the sticker underneath).
You just documented a **test environment** for the first time — the habit behind every
bug report you'll ever write.
</Challenge>
